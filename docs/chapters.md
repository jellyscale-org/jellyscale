# Chapters

A film's or episode's chapters (names and start positions) are read from Matroska and MP4 files by the scan, in the same ffprobe run that lists the streams, and sent as `Chapters` in the item as Jellyfin 12.4 sends them: a name (`Chapter 3` for an untitled one) and a start position on the file's clock. In jellyfin-web that gives a marker on the seek bar for each chapter and working next and previous chapter buttons, in direct play and in a transcode. Its details page lists chapters only when they have pictures, so by default it lists none, as Jellyfin 12.4 doesn't with its option off (`compat/captures/chapters-12.4.md`).

## Chapter pictures

Turn them on per library, in the config, which is off by default as in Jellyfin:

```json
{"name": "Movies", "kind": "movies", "media": "s3://films?...", "assets": "s3://assets?...", "chapterImages": true}
```

After each scan a worker takes one frame at the start of each chapter of each film and episode of that library, in one job per file (`subtitle_jobs`, kind `chapters`, key `chapters/{source}`), two files at a time per worker, on slots that subtitle extraction and transcodes don't share, so a library full of them doesn't hold up a viewer's subtitle. The pictures are kept in the library's asset store under `chapters/{source}/`, at most 400 pixels wide (what jellyfin-web asks for). A chapter gets its `ImageTag` only once its job has finished, so the details page stays as it was while pictures are made and fills in all at once; `GET /Items/{id}/Images/Chapter/{n}` is served by the `stream` role as stored (`maxWidth` and `quality` are ignored, as for the other item images, since that role has no ffmpeg), and any index that is not a chapter with a picture is a 404. An HDR film's pictures are tone mapped as playback is. A file without a video stream is not queued. Turning the option on for a library already scanned queues its files at the next scan. Upgrading: roll workers first, since a worker from before this release that claims a chapter job fails it.

**When storage is reclaimed.** The scan queues the job of every file with work, whether the option is on or off, and the job deletes everything under `chapters/{source}/` that is not the pictures of the file as it is now. So the pictures of a file are deleted by the next scan's job after the option is turned off, after its chapters vanish, after the file changes (its pictures are made again under a new directory) and after the file is removed (the job finds the library's asset store from its own record, which outlives the file). A job that was overtaken by a replaced file leaves pictures that the next job deletes in the same way, and so does a job that failed midway. When a file's attempts are used up, the next job gives up: it deletes the pictures made so far and records the file as done with none, so nothing is left behind. Turning the option off, letting a scan run, and turning it on again deletes the job's record and so also resets the cap below; turning it back on before a scan has run changes nothing. Renaming or dropping a library, or changing its asset store URI, leaves its old pictures where they are.

**The cap.** Making starts at most 3 times for one version of a file; the count is in the picture record (`chapter_images`), not in the job, so the daily sweep of finished jobs does not reset it. Each scan queues a failed job again. The job itself checks the count, so a job a worker takes over after another's lease lapsed is held to it too, and the count is taken before anything is read, so a job that fails at once (an asset store it can't list) counts as well. A job that finds the count at 3 gives up as above, and the file then stays without pictures until it changes (or the option is turned off for a scan and on again). No attempt is ever given back: a worker that stops while it is making, or an asset or media store that is down, uses attempts like a bad file does, so three scans of an outage (about 45 minutes at the default scan interval) leave the films without pictures. The give-up is logged with this cause and the remedy: turn the library's chapter images off and on again across two scans. Deleting has no cap: it is tried again at every scan until it works.

**What fails a job and what skips a chapter.** A chapter whose frame cannot be read is skipped for good for that version of the file, and the others keep their pictures: ffmpeg exits with an error (a decode error), the frame is past the end of the file, or one frame takes more than 2 minutes. The last means that a slow store can leave chapters without pictures until the file changes, and cost 2 minutes of one slot per such chapter. After any frame fails, the job checks that the store can still give the file's first byte, within 30 seconds; a store that can't fails the job instead (an attempt), and that chapter is not recorded as skipped, since the store and not the file failed it, so the next attempt reads it again; a stalled store doesn't turn its chapters into skips, and the check itself gives up after 30 seconds even on a POSIX mount whose reads hang. A frame read from such a mount is bounded by its 2-minute deadline only if the kill ends ffmpeg: a mount whose reads ignore even SIGKILL (a hard NFS mount, say) holds that chapter slot until the mount answers. The job fails instead, and is retried by later scans up to the cap, on an error of the asset store or of the media store while ffmpeg reads it (including a file the store can no longer read at all), ffmpeg not starting, or ffmpeg killed by a signal (the OOM killer, say), which says something about the worker and not the file. A worker that is stopping puts its job back in the queue for another worker, and a worker that lost its lease leaves the job to the one that took it over; either way the attempt it started stays counted. Each chapter's outcome is recorded as the job goes, so a later attempt starts after the chapters already handled and never reads a skipped one again. jellyfin-web's details page lists chapters only if the first has a picture, so a film whose first chapter was skipped shows no chapter list, though its other pictures are kept and served.

**Cost**, per film, once: one ffmpeg run for each chapter, each opening the file and seeking to the chapter (from S3, the header and index and the group of pictures at that position, as a few ranged GETs), and a stored picture of 9 to 43 kB at 400 pixels. Measured with ffmpeg 9.0.2: the job's own command (`-seek_timestamp 1 -ss T`, `scale` to at most 400 wide, `-q:v 3`, MJPEG) on three 4 s, 1920x1080, 25 fps sources made by `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i NAME=size=1920x1080:rate=25 -t 4 -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p`, frames at 1 s, 2 s and 3 s. `testsrc2` gave 9,337, 9,386 and 9,773 bytes, `mandelbrot` 12,254, 14,017 and 15,831, and `life` (noise-like) 43,417, 42,603 and 41,630. These are synthetic sources and no real film was measured, so a grainy film may be larger than the noisiest of them; size a bucket with a margin. At 43 kB, a film with 30 chapters is 30 seeks and 1.3 MB and a library of 1000 such films is 30,000 seeks into the NAS or the bucket and 1.3 GB of assets (0.3 GB at 10 kB), spread over each worker's two jobs at a time and the scan interval. Nothing is read again until the file changes. A film whose chapters have no picture yet behaves as with the option off.

Upgrading: the first scan after this version probes again every film stored before it, once, to read its chapters: one ffprobe open of the file (as from S3 the scan's first probe did), plus a poster frame for a film with no poster image beside it, but not the keyframe index, which is the expensive part for H.264, HEVC and AV1 files and is kept. A film with no chapters is not probed again after that. Roll in any order: an older replica ignores the new column, and its scan leaves the chapters unread, so the next scan by a new one reads them.
