# Status

Where jellyscale stands against [PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md), which says what it should be. This file is the record of what is built, partial and not built, and of the route audit against Jellyfin 12.4; `go test ./compat` checks it against `compat/coverage-12.4.tsv`. The work still to do is in [tasks/prd.json](../tasks/prd.json).

## Roadmap and slices

Subtitles inside the file are delivered or burnt in (done for text tracks, extracted by workers so that `api` and `stream` need no ffmpeg); external subtitle files beside the film (SubRip, ASS, SSA, WebVTT, and PGS and VobSub image files) are done the same way; image tracks inside the film (PGS, VobSub, DVB) are listed as Jellyfin lists them, and PGS is sent as the file to a client that draws it (jellyfin-web with its PGS setting on) and, like VobSub and DVB, drawn into a transcode otherwise, with `api` and `stream` still running no media tools; a run that begins mid-film draws the cue showing at its cut (DOMAINS 51), from the track's own stored copy once a worker has made it (JS-089). External `.sup` and `.sub`/`.idx` files are built, and a VobSub pair is drawn into a transcode, never sent as a file, as Jellyfin does for a file. When the client plays a film's video but not its audio or container, the video is copied and the audio is copied too if the client takes its codec, else re-encoded to stereo AAC (done for H.264, HEVC and AV1 in MKV and MP4), so the picture keeps its quality exactly and the operator spends almost no CPU. A copy at Auto carries the file's whole bitrate and size, 4K included, as direct play does, and a viewer on a slow link picks a cap. Its segments are stored at the file's own bitrate, not under the re-encode's ceiling, and a worker opens the file once when it copies both streams and reads it at 5 times the film's speed, so five times the film's bitrate while the job runs and its bytes once for the whole film (twice each when the audio is re-encoded), until the film ends or a minute after the last request, and never more than `transcodeLead` (60 seconds of film by default) ahead of the viewer; a seek waits for a whole segment, up to 12 seconds of film. When the video is re-encoded, the audio is copied if the client's fMP4 HLS profile lists its codec (AAC, MP3, AC3, E-AC3, Opus or FLAC) (a real client has played only AAC, Opus and FLAC; the rest are checked with ffmpeg only), its channels fit the profile's limit and it takes no more than a quarter of the bitrate cap (a deliberate choice: a copy that took more would starve the picture); a copy costs a worker no audio encode and the network and storage of the track's own bitrate (up to 640 kbps for AC3, 1 to 4 Mbps for FLAC) in place of 192 kbps. A restart on another worker puts the same audio packets in the same segments: for an MP4 source the audio in each segment is byte for byte the same, and for a Matroska source the packets are identical but their times can differ by under 1 ms, because Matroska stores whole milliseconds (accepted, not a defect). A missing, non-numeric or out-of-range `TranscodingMaxAudioChannels` is 2. When the client plays both the video and the audio codec but not the container, both are copied (copying both is built, DOMAINS 48 and 52): the picture and sound are the file's own packets and the audio is the track the viewer picked. The scan reads a keyframe index from each H.264 file (a Matroska file's Cues, an MP4's moov, which for a large file is megabytes), and reads where the video ends from the last stretch of the file. Phases and gates are in the project plan. In short: read-only browse (done), direct play (done), transcode (re-encode, subtitles and video copy done), HA, library and metadata, Kubernetes, SyncPlay.

Slices, in delivery order. The slice names are the ones in the `slice` column of `compat/coverage-12.4.tsv`, and `go test ./compat` fails if a needed route names another.

1. `phase3`: the remainder of Phase 3, the transcode gaps named above and in Features and Known gaps.
2. `phase4-ha`: Phase 4, high availability, with the gate below. Remote control of sessions rides on its cross-replica delivery.
3. `identity`: user management, hashed tokens, Quick Connect, then OIDC beside local accounts, right after HA.
4. `phase5-library`: Phase 5, library and metadata.
5. `phase6-k8s`: Phase 6, Kubernetes, with metrics and upgrades.
6. `phase7-syncplay`: Phase 7, SyncPlay.

Gate for Phase 4 (HA): a worker, and an API replica, killed in the middle of a film recover without losing the viewer's picture or resume position. The worker half is built: workers hold a Postgres advisory lock that dies with their process, the other workers notice it gone within a quarter second and gives the job to a live worker at the first missing segment, and in jellyfin-web the picture goes on with no dialog and no failed request (killed 1.6 seconds into playback, the least buffer there is, the picture of a re-encode stood still 2.5 to 2.8 seconds and that of a copy 0.3; a Go test with no browser measured about 4 seconds from SIGKILL to the replacement's first segment, and hls.js abandons a request at 10; `DOMAINS.md` invariants 25, 37 and 38). It needs a second worker, or the same one restarted within six seconds. When the lock is not closed with the process (host power loss, a partition, a connection pooler that holds the server's end), the dead job keeps its lease, up to 10 seconds from each renewal, and the picture freezes with no dialog; it is slow, and the worker stores nothing once its lease is gone. A burnt-in subtitle whose extraction was on the killed worker waits for that extraction's own 10 second lease. A viewer already playing an H.264, HEVC or AV1 video copy whose keyframe index is found wrong goes on as a capped re-encode under the playlist it holds (`DOMAINS.md` invariant 47); an AV1 copy's fallback is an SVT-AV1 encode (JS-133). The API replica half is built (JS-031): an `api` replica killed mid-film loses neither the picture nor the resume position, a stream replica killed or rolled mid-film is replaced by another for HLS and direct play, and a replica stopped with SIGTERM fails readiness first and then drains.

## Features

Every viewer-visible and operator-visible feature in scope, in one list. Status is **done**, **partial** or **not built**, and a feature is grouped under the slice that delivers it. A feature that is a non-goal is listed in the last group with a reason. Items marked (decision) are choices a maintainer should confirm.

### Built

- Browse libraries, items, seasons and episodes, latest and continue watching, and favourites: **done**.
- Played state through `/UserPlayedItems`, `POST /UserItems/{itemId}/UserData` (a partial write of played, position, last played and favourite; a body whose `LastPlayedDate` is older than the stored one cannot undo a newer position or played state, `DOMAINS.md` invariant 49) and playback reporting: **done**.
- Direct play from POSIX and S3-compatible stores, with ranges and HEAD: **done**.
- Transcode to H.264 with audio track and bitrate choice, seeks, and text subtitles delivered or burnt in: **done**.
- Video copy with audio re-encoded (SDR H.264, HEVC and AV1, in MKV and MP4; HDR10, HDR10+ and HLG HEVC and AV1 for a client that shows the range, else tone mapped): **done**.
- Parallel transcoding: a re-encode running below 1.2x real time gets helper jobs on other workers for the 4-segment chunks ahead of the viewer, and a new viewer who finds no worker with the cores its job costs stops the helpers that hold them, of one worker, rather than waiting; `transcodeHelpers: false` turns it off (`docs/running.md`): **done** (a burnt image subtitle gets helpers too, as its chunks draw the cue showing; helpers use a fixed threshold, not the cluster's spare capacity). A helper whose every viewer has seeked away (forward beyond it, or far back) is stopped within a second or so and gives its worker back, counted as `helper_left_behind` (JS-167); measured on three 2-core workers in jellyfin-web, a 4K HDR re-encode then holds real time after a seek to the middle and back, with and without a burned-in PGS track (docs/transcoding.md). JS-168 keeps no room back for a viewer who has not asked: a helper goes to any worker with its cores free, and a new viewer on a full cluster stops a helper (D-046). Measured on two 2-core workers in jellyfin-web (docs/transcoding.md), the film holds 1.00x for 10 minutes before and after a seek to the middle, with and without a burned-in PGS track, and so does a 2-core worker beside a 6-core one (one run disturbed by the machine's load fell to 0.98x and was repeated within the bound). Freezes come only in the first 21 s after a start or a seek, but up to 7.5 s on a busy machine, over the 2 s bound: the bound is kept and JS-174 measures it on a quiet machine (D-051). A second viewer arriving then starts as fast as on an idle cluster, and the first viewer falls to about 0.8x for as long as they watch; JS-161 refuses, with its reason, a newcomer who would fit only by stopping a watched film's helper (D-050). No start or seek is slower for a lone viewer.
- Local accounts with bcrypt passwords and per-device tokens; the first administrator comes from configuration: **partial** (no user management, see `identity`).
- Sessions list and playback reporting: **partial** (listing and progress done, no operator view of devices).
- Items' multiple audio tracks: **done**. The details page's audio menu lists every audio track the viewer has not hidden (JS-147, D-018), and plays a pick as a copy of the video with that track; where a pick cannot be delivered (no transcode store, no probed duration, no worker that runs transcodes, or the roles' stores differ) it lists the viewer's track alone, and a pick that cannot be delivered is refused, never played as another track (D-029, DOMAINS 32).
- Search by term (jellyfin-web's search page calls `GET /Items` with `searchTerm`): **done**.

### `phase3`

- HEVC and AV1 to clients that play them: **done** as direct play and as a copy into fMP4 HLS (DOMAINS 45), for HEVC Main and Main 10 and AV1 profile 0 at 8 or 10 bits, SDR, when the client's DeviceProfile lists the codec. Anything else is re-encoded to H.264 as before. Dolby Vision with an HDR10, HLG or SDR base layer is copied to a client that plays the base layer (JS-023). HDR10, HDR10+ and HLG are copied to a client whose profile shows the range (JS-025). Encoding to AV1 (SVT-AV1) and to HEVC (x265) is built for a copy's fallback only (JS-133, JS-088; an HDR copy's fallback keeps its range at 10 bits, JS-134). Not built: encoding to either as the first choice for a client. CI's Chrome decodes AV1 but not HEVC, so a browser plays HEVC only on a machine with a hardware decoder (`E2E_HEVC=1`).
- Copying audio while the video is re-encoded: AAC, Opus and FLAC **done** (played and sought in jellyfin-web in Chromium); AC3, E-AC3 and MP3 **partial**: copied for clients whose profile lists them, checked with ffmpeg only. Copying both the video and the audio: **done** (a client that takes the track's codec in fMP4 HLS, within its channel limit and a quarter of the cap; otherwise the audio is re-encoded and the video still copied); played in jellyfin-web in Chromium with AAC, and for AC3, E-AC3, MP3, Opus and FLAC checked with ffmpeg only. A copy of both opens the file once.
- HDR tone mapping to SDR: **done** (DOMAINS 53; jellyfin-web plays HDR10 and HLG HEVC and 10-bit H.264 films through it in Chromium). A re-encoded film in PQ or HLG (HDR10, HLG, and Dolby Vision with an HDR10 or HLG base layer, whose own data is ignored) is tone mapped to SDR BT.709 by a worker in software, with `zscale` and `tonemap` (hable), which every supported ffmpeg has. An HDR film is never copied to a client that was told the video is SDR, H.264 included; HDR10, HDR10+ and HLG HEVC and AV1 are copied, and HDR is direct played, for a client whose profile shows the range, the master saying `VIDEO-RANGE=PQ` or `HLG` (JS-025). It costs 2.7 times the CPU of an SDR re-encode from a 1080p source and 2.0 times from a 4K one (3.2 cores to keep a 4K source in real time, measured, DOMAINS 53), and a worker's cores are weighed by it (a 4K tone mapped job costs 3.2). Not built: an SDR variant beside an HDR copy in the master, GPU tone mapping. Dolby Vision profile 5, which has no HDR10 base, is re-encoded to H.264, whether its colours come out right is not observed, and JS-149 settles it (D-021).
- All text tracks inside a film extracted in one pass (JS-028): **done**. The first request for any text track has one worker run one ffmpeg over the film that writes every text track, and each is kept in the asset store, so a later pick of another track starts no job and reads nothing (on S3, the range proxy shows the film read once). Two viewers on different api processes asking for different tracks of one film at the same moment cause one read. The job is the film's (`subtitle_jobs` key `subtitles/{source}/{etag hash}/text`), leased and taken over after a worker's death as a track job was. Tracks ffmpeg can't extract (it names the streams) are left out, the pass is run again without all of them (two reads of the film in all, however many; tracks the file lacks are dropped first, before ffmpeg reads the film), each run with the whole time limit; a run that ends at a limit is followed by one run for each text track not yet stored, so a film of n text tracks costs at most 2 + n reads in all (1 + n when the pass named no stream), and each is marked unreadable in the film's streams, only if the file is still the version the pass read, until the file changes, so it answers 500 on its own and costs no read afterwards; a failure that names no stream (a file ffmpeg can't open, a timeout) is remembered for 30 seconds for all the film's text tracks and marks nothing. PGS tracks (`sup`), fonts and files beside the film keep their own jobs. See `docs/subtitles.md`.
- Image subtitles inside the file (PGS, VobSub, DVB): **done**. They are listed as Jellyfin lists them (`Codec` `PGSSUB`, `DVDSUB`, `DVBSUB`), and any other bitmap codec is not listed. jellyfin-web 12.4 draws PGS itself from the file when its viewer turns on PGS rendering (a setting that is off by default): the track is `External`, a worker copies it out of the film once (the whole film is read once for each PGS track) and `stream` sends it from the asset store. For every other viewer, and for VobSub and DVB always, choosing the track is a transcode with the track drawn into the picture, which a worker's ffmpeg reads from the film in the same pass as the video. A run that begins mid-film (a seek, a failover, a helper's chunk) draws the cue showing at its cut: the scan keeps where the track's packets lie from the Matroska Cues (no read of the whole film), and a run reads the packet of the cue showing and opens the film at its first packet, so a cue's age is paid for in extra reading by a run inside it until the track is stored (DOMAINS 51). The first play that burns an image track inside the film has one worker job read the whole film once for each version of the file, also when the viewer stops early, and store each such track as a stored track of its own in the asset store (JS-089, D-003: a Matroska file of that one track, about the track's own size, and one `subtitle_jobs` row for the film); a run that begins mid-film then draws the cue showing at its cut from it, as a second input with the film opened at its cut, and reads a few kilobytes of it whatever the cue's age and the film's length, and nothing more of the film than a run without the track. Until the track is stored (not asked for yet, being extracted, or failed), or when its copy ran out the hour and is retried by a play at most once a day (D-002, a gap listed below), a run reads the film as before, and a track that cannot be extracted for the film's own reason stays listed and burnt as before and is not read again until the file changes. A film with no cue points for the track (MPEG-TS, a Matroska file written without Cues for the track) is opened differently until its track is stored: a run there opens a 10 second look-back early and an older cue is missed until the next, which a stored track closes. VobSub inside the film is not sent as a file (`.mks`) to the clients that draw it; it is drawn into a transcode (a deliberate difference, DOMAINS 51). Not built: choosing a track by the viewer's subtitle mode (above).
- External subtitle files beside the film (`Film (2024).en.forced.srt`, `.ass`, `.ssa`, `.vtt`, from POSIX and S3): **done**. They are listed after the film's own tracks, in file name order, with the language (ISO 639-3), `Forced`, `Default` and hearing-impaired flags (`forced`, `foreign`, `default`, `cc`, `hi`, `sdh`) and any other word as `Title` that Jellyfin 12.4 gives them, and are delivered and burnt in through the path of an embedded track (`DOMAINS.md` invariant 50). The scan only lists them; a worker converts a file to UTF-8 when it is first asked for (UTF-8, UTF-16, and the legacy sets chardet detects, with the language in the file's name breaking a near-tie; Windows-1250 text and Thai are known to be misread, and the remedy is to convert the file to UTF-8), and a file that none of chardet's results decodes, or that ffmpeg rejects, is hidden with a warning until it changes, once the first request for it has found that out (that viewer gets an error, and a burn-in of it freezes the picture until a seek or reload); a failure that says nothing about the file (ffmpeg killed by a signal, a full disk, the store not answering) leaves it listed to be tried again. A rescan notices a file added, changed or removed without probing the film again; a file keeps its index while it exists and the index of a removed file is never reused, so open sessions are not shifted. It differs from Jellyfin in numbering the film's own streams first, where Jellyfin numbers the external files first and shifts the film's indices, and in reading only `.srt`, `.ass`, `.ssa`, `.vtt`, `.sup` and `.sub` with `.idx` (Jellyfin also takes `.smi`, and lists a `.sub` with no `.idx`). Which track a viewer starts with is not decided by the server (see `identity`).
- External image subtitle files beside the film (`.sup` for PGS, and a `.sub` with its `.idx` for VobSub, from POSIX and S3): **done**, as `compat/captures/external-image-subtitles-12.4.md` observed on Jellyfin 12.4. They are listed in file name order with the text files (a pair at its `.idx`), with the flags from the file name and the canvas from the file; a pair's `Language` is the one inside the `.idx` (`en`, two letters, as Jellyfin lists it), not ISO 639-3. A `.sub` with no `.idx`, an `.idx` with no `.sub` and an `.idx` that holds no `id:` line are not listed and do not stop the scan. The scan reads only these small files (the `.sup`'s first segment and the `.idx`), never the film. jellyfin-web with its PGS setting on gets a `.sup` as `External` and `stream` sends the file itself from the media store (as `Stream.pgssub` or `Stream.sup`, with ranges) with no worker job; every other client, and VobSub always, gets `Encode`, and the worker copies the file (or both files of a pair) to its scratch directory and draws it as a second ffmpeg input, read from its start so a cue showing at a seek is drawn.
- Font attachments of Matroska files for ASS rendering (`GET /Videos/{videoId}/{mediaSourceId}/Attachments/{index}`): **done**. The scan lists them; the first PlaybackInfo or ASS track request has a worker take all the film's fonts out in one job and keep them in the asset store, and `MediaAttachments` lists the fonts once they are stored (until then the list is empty and jellyfin-web draws ASS in its fallback font). The `DeliveryUrl` carries the caller's token as a subtitle's does, where Jellyfin's carries none and its route needs none. A burnt-in ASS or SSA track waits for the same job and is drawn with the film's fonts too (a SubRip or WebVTT burn does not wait on it); if the fonts can't be had for a reason that may pass (the store, a signal, a full disk), the burn fails and is asked for again, because a burn without them would be kept for 24 hours; a film whose attachments ffmpeg rejects is remembered as having none, per film version, and is burnt without them.
- The subtitle HLS playlist (`GET /Videos/{itemId}/{mediaSourceId}/Subtitles/{index}/subtitles.m3u8?segmentLength=N`, for a client whose profile offers the `Hls` subtitle method) and the subtitle route without a start offset (`.../Subtitles/{index}/Stream.{format}`): **done**, as observed on Jellyfin 12.4 (`compat/captures/subtitles-12.4.md`): a VOD playlist with one WebVTT segment per `segmentLength` seconds of the film, each a request for `stream.vtt` with the cues of its window and the caller's token as `ApiKey`. jellyfin-web's profile sends `External` alone and never asks for it. It differs from Jellyfin in answering 404 for a track that does not exist, where Jellyfin answers a playlist.
- File download (`GET /Items/{itemId}/Download`, from POSIX and S3 through the direct-play path, on the `stream` role and the ingress's stream rule; items say `CanDownload` true so jellyfin-web offers it): **done**. `Content-Disposition` carries both `filename` and `filename*`, as Jellyfin does. It differs from Jellyfin in answering HEAD like GET (Jellyfin gives 405) and in taking a token in every form the server reads, including `X-Emby-Token` and `api_key`, which Jellyfin 12.4 refuses with 401 (`DOMAINS.md` invariant 49).
- A transcode job runs at most `transcodeLead` (default 60 s of film) ahead of the furthest segment its viewers have asked for, then its ffmpeg pauses until they come within the lead (DOMAINS 57): **done**, for re-encodes and copies, across replicas, tested with real processes and with a copy from SeaweedFS and Garage paused for 20 s, and in jellyfin-web in Chromium (plays, seeks, switches audio with the bound on). A viewer who leaves after a minute of a 40 Mbps film costs about 1.6 GB of reading instead of 6 GB (computed, assuming the player asks 30 s beyond what it shows; docs/transcoding.md). Jellyfin 12.4 does not bound by default (`EnableThrottling` false).
- Chapters, names and start positions: **done** (DOMAINS 54). The scan reads them from Matroska and MP4 (including QuickTime chapter tracks) in the ffprobe run that lists the streams, and the item DTO carries `Chapters` as Jellyfin 12.4 does: `Name` (`Chapter N` for an untitled one), `StartPositionTicks` on the file's clock. jellyfin-web 12.4 then shows a marker for each on the seek bar and its next and previous chapter buttons seek to them, in direct play and in a transcode (`test/e2e`).
- Chapter images: **done** (DOMAINS 55), opt-in per library (`chapterImages`, off by default as in Jellyfin 12.4; `compat/captures/chapter-images-12.4.md`). A worker takes a frame at each chapter's start after the scan, in a job with slots of its own, keeps it in the asset store, and the item's chapters carry `ImageTag` once it exists; `GET /Items/{id}/Images/Chapter/{n}` serves it from the stream role. jellyfin-web's details page then lists the chapters with their pictures and picking one starts the film there, in direct play and in a transcode (`test/e2e`). With the option off the details page lists none, as Jellyfin's does. Not built: resizing (the pictures are 400 pixels wide, as jellyfin-web asks), `ImagePath`, Jellyfin's split between "during the scan" and a scheduled task.
- `POST /UserItems/{itemId}/UserData`, which writes played state and favourites as one record (Streamyfin's offline sync; a name match, not a trace): **done**. `GET` on the same path is out of scope until something is found that calls it.

### `phase4-ha`

- A worker killed mid-film recovers without losing the picture or resume position, another worker taking its job within seconds: **done**. An API replica or a stream replica killed mid-film, and a replica stopped with SIGTERM: **done** (JS-031, the gate above).
- Remote control of another session (play on another device, messages): **not built**. In scope because jellyfin-web offers it (decision).

### `identity`

- User management: create, delete, disable, change a password: **not built**. In scope.
- A viewer's playback settings: **done** (saved), through `POST /Users/Configuration` and the obsolete `POST /Users/{userId}/Configuration`, and listed by `GET /Localization/Cultures` (`compat/captures/user-configuration-12.4.md`). All 16 fields of `UserConfiguration` come back in every answer that carries the user. AudioLanguagePreference and PlayDefaultAudioTrack are acting (JS-121): they choose the track a film starts with, in PlaybackInfo's and the item's `DefaultAudioStreamIndex`, as Jellyfin 12.4 does (`compat/captures/audio-default-12.4.md`), and a track that is not the file's default is a copied stream for a browser (docs/transcoding.md). SubtitleMode and SubtitleLanguagePreference change no track choice yet (JS-041).
- A viewer's track preferences (JS-118): **done** (saved), through `GET` and `PATCH /jellyscale/v1/track-preferences`, the first routes of jellyscale's own namespace (docs/api.md). They are stored per viewer in `users.track_preferences`, apart from `UserConfiguration`, and no Jellyfin answer carries them.
- Every track, each marked (JS-120): **done**, through `POST /jellyscale/v1/items/{itemId}/playback-info`, PlaybackInfo with every audio and subtitle track listed as itself and a `Hidden` mark on each by the rule JS-119 hides tracks by, and `OriginalLanguage` on each source (docs/api.md).
- A client saves one setting without overwriting the rest (JS-123): **done**, through `PATCH /jellyscale/v1/users/configuration`, which changes only the fields it names, merged in one statement (docs/api.md). Jellyfin's two POST routes are as before.
- A film recovers from a failed segment without the viewer acting (JS-088): **done** for a failure that is not the file's, and for a copy whose keyframe index is wrong; and for an AV1 copy (JS-133: an SVT-AV1 encode, one ffmpeg for each segment, since an H.264 segment cannot follow an AV1 SourceBuffer); **not built** for a copy fallback while Postgres is away (a snapshot session answers only what the store holds). jellyfin-web, observed (`compat/captures/segment-errors-12.4.md`): after playback has begun a segment answered 500 or 404 ends the stream (no new PlaybackInfo, no dialog, the picture freezes), one with no first byte for 10 s is asked for again, a connection closed mid-body is asked for again too; ExoPlayer (Media3) is read from its source and not observed. A job that failed for a cause that is not the file's (the transcode store refusing a put, the source store cutting a read, ffmpeg killed by a signal; `transcode_jobs.again`) is made again as the same kind of job for the request that waited on it, at most `maxRestarts` (3) times for one request, and it refuses neither the spec nor PlaybackInfo. A copy whose index a job finds wrong keeps the viewer's playlist: segments the store holds are the copy's, the rest are made by an x264 (H.264 copy), x265 (HEVC copy) or SVT-AV1 (AV1 copy) encode cut on the playlist's own layout (`playback.Spec.Recode`, `dropped_keyframes`), each sent with its init segment in front. The log says `a copied stream falls back to a re-encode` with the item, source and reason, and `jellyscale_playback_copy_fallbacks_total` counts each stream once. The encode is capped at 1080 lines like any re-encode. The x265 encode is priced at the x264 row plus up to 2.4 cores (measured on `testsrc2`, not on a film), has no helpers, and the AV1 encode is priced from the CPU of its own ffmpeg on 6.1 and 9 (docs/transcoding.md). A cause that persists starts at most three fresh jobs for a request and is the spec's remembered failure across requests (a count in SQL of the `again` failures within 30 seconds). `OutputVersion` is 7 (6 added the AV1 fallback, 7 the HDR copy's fallback that keeps the picture's range), which a worker of 5 cannot make (docs/running.md).
- Hiding tracks outside a viewer's languages (JS-119): **done**, per viewer and opt-in. PlaybackInfo and one item's details list an audio or subtitle track outside the viewer's languages as a placeholder of Type `Data` at its own Index, so no index moves; jellyfin-web leaves it out of its menus and media info. Kept: the viewer's languages, the film's own language, the languages they keep, every track with no language and the track the request names. Nothing is removed or extracted, and every index is still accepted. Limit: language families other than Norwegian and Chinese compare as different languages; the remedy is the keep list or "show all tracks" (docs/subtitles.md, DOMAINS.md invariant 62).
- Hashed tokens at rest and operator API keys: **not built**. In scope.
- Per-user library access: **not built**. In scope.
- Quick Connect: **not built**. In scope; apps and TVs sign in through it.
- `POST /Users/ForgotPassword`: **not built**. In scope; it answers `Action: ContactAdmin`, because the operator resets passwords (`/Users/ForgotPassword/Pin` is a non-goal).
- OIDC beside local accounts: **not built**. In scope. In jellyfin-web it is a link on the login page, carried by the branding login disclaimer (so `GET /Branding/Configuration` stops being an empty stub); apps and TVs use Quick Connect. Capture real clients before building it (decision).
- Signing the URLs inside playlists, so that they stop carrying the client's own token as `ApiKey` (`DOMAINS.md` invariant 17): **not built**. Known exposure today: anyone who can read a playlist, or a log of the segment requests, holds the viewer's token. In scope.
- Starting on the default subtitle per the viewer's subtitle mode (`SubtitleMode` None, Default, Always, OnlyForced, Smart, and `SubtitleLanguagePreference`): **not built**. In scope. PlaybackInfo reports `DefaultSubtitleStreamIndex` -1 unless the client names a track, as it did for tracks inside the film, and the details page does not carry it. ### `phase5-library`

- Library scanning from configured libraries with keyframe index and probe: **partial** (done for films and episodes, no metadata providers).
- Capacity weighted by what a job costs (JS-087, `transcodeCores`, `playback.Spec.Cost`, docs/transcoding.md): **done**. A worker declares cores; a re-encode costs 0.8, 1.6, 2.0 or 3.2 by its source, a copy 0.05 under a bound of its own; PlaybackInfo offers a transcode when one worker has the cost free, and the queue signal is in cores (`jellyscale_transcode_queue_cores`). A worker row beside the release before keeps a positive `slots`.
- One typed media model (JS-086, `internal/media`): **done**: the probe, the scan, the playback decision, the subtitle code, the transcode worker and every answer share one `MediaSource`, `MediaStream` and `MediaAttachment` type, which sends each field `compat/schema-12.4.json` requires and only the others a source has. A source stores the integer version of the probe that wrote it (`media_sources.probe_version`, `library.ProbeVersion`); a scan probes an unchanged file again when its source is older, keeping the item, the subtitle files' indexes and a stored keyframe index. Raise `ProbeVersion` when a release probes more or differently. A probe that fails leaves the source as it was, and the next scan tries again.
- A film's original language (JS-117): **done**. It comes from the file alone, with no internet and no metadata provider: the language of the default audio track (the first flagged default, else the first), and none when that track has no language (untagged, und, mis, mul, zxx, unparseable), never the next tagged track. `GET /Items/{itemId}` answers it as `OriginalLanguage`, the tag as the file wrote it, and PlaybackInfo and the item mark `IsOriginal` on every audio stream in that language (`playback.SameLanguage`, which compares languages, not spellings); lists answer neither. It is made when the item is answered, so no rescan or migration is needed. A film whose only audio is a dub is labelled with the dub's language.
- Every playback decision is logged and counted with its reasons (JS-153): **done**. Each source a PlaybackInfo answer lists writes one `playback decision` line at Info (a refused answer one line, `method=refused`, with one reason; an answer that gets no reply none) with the method (`direct_play`, `copy` or `reencode`), every reason from one vocabulary (`playback.Reasons`, listed in docs/running.md, Jellyfin's names where it has them), the client, the device, the account's id, the item, the source and the play session, what the viewer gets (codec, height, range, tone mapped, whether the 1080 line ceiling lowered it) and the audio and subtitle the request asked for and the ones that play. No title, file name or path is in it. `jellyscale_playbackinfo_refusals_total` gains a `reason` label and `jellyscale_playbackinfo_decision_reasons_total{method,reason}` counts the reasons of each decision; all three are on the api process's `/metrics`. The reasons only the line holds (a keyframe index that cannot cut segments, the track pick) are in no TranscodeReasons, and the reply, the TranscodingUrl and `GET /Sessions` are what they were. A MediaSourceId in upper case names its source now (it listed none before).

## Known gaps

What does not yet work for every film or viewer, each with what the viewer or operator gets today and what closes it: an open task, 'no task' with the decision that accepted it, or 'no task yet' with the decision and the open task that will decide it.

- A gap: on a worker as slow as the dogfood's (4 cores, i5-6500, about 2.7 reference cores) a 4K HDR or Dolby Vision film that must be re-encoded, with or without a burned-in subtitle, costs 3.2 reference cores, more than the worker has: a lone worker with a declared capacity (`transcodeCores`, set to the computed figure) refuses such a play within seconds with its reason (`NoWorkerWithFreeCores`, naming the job's cost and the most any worker has), where it was admitted before and stalled at about 0.85 times real time; two such workers share it with helpers (JS-161, D-044, D-046), and JS-166 runs it on the dogfood. An undeclared worker counts its processors at the reference speed and overstates itself on a slow CPU, so it is admitted and stalls as before, which the assumed gauge and the unhelped line say (D-062). Until then such films play only on a client that takes them as they are, and the dialog says why.
- A gap: a film whose picture is re-encoded comes out H.264, SDR, at no more than 1080 lines, a 4K or HDR film and one with a burned-in image subtitle included, so a viewer on a 4K or HDR screen gets 1080p SDR from it. Direct play and a copy are not capped; a copy's fallback, HEVC or AV1 (10 bit for an HDR copy), is capped at 1080 lines like any re-encode. No task yet: JS-148 measures what a taller or HDR re-encode costs and how often the dogfood's viewers get a capped one, and its brief decides the closer (D-021).
- A gap: whether a re-encoded Dolby Vision profile 5 film's picture comes out right is not observed, and the documents disagreed about it; the film is re-encoded to H.264 for every client that does not declare Dolby Vision, and the viewer gets whatever that picture is. JS-149 observes it and makes or pins it right (D-021).
- A gap: a Dolby Vision profile 5 film is re-encoded, never copied or direct played, for every client that does not declare Dolby Vision, because its base layer is neither HDR10 nor HLG; the viewer gets a re-encode and the operator pays its CPU. No task (D-021).
- A gap, not supported: an app whose player sends no token on a media request is answered 401 and cannot play it. Today that is Findroid's direct play, Android TV's direct play (the player table below), and Wholphin's (its ExoPlayer player), Jellyfin for Roku's and JellyCon's, each read from source ('Which apps send their token' below). A viewer of such an app gets no film from it, where Jellyfin serves the same request to anyone (`compat/captures/access-12.4.md`), and uses jellyfin-web, Octave or an app that sends its token. No task (D-026, amending D-005): every media route needs a token, and no work goes in to make such an app play; it plays with no change to jellyscale once it sends one.
- A gap: a film whose image subtitles cannot be copied within the hour keeps seeks into a long-showing cue that cost more the older the cue, and the copy is retried only by a play and at most once a day, or at once when the file changes (D-002); the viewer is not told. JS-140 closes it.
- A gap: during a Postgres switchover or restart some viewer requests are answered 500, a subtitle can be refused for 30 s after a read that hit the outage, and a film started from the snapshot ignores the viewer's languages. JS-146 closes it.

## Feature audit against Jellyfin 12.4

`compat/coverage-12.4.tsv` has one row for each operation in Jellyfin 12.4.0's stable OpenAPI document (`https://repo.jellyfin.org/files/openapi/stable/jellyfin-openapi-12.4.json`, 364 operations: 346 plus 18 HEAD operations the document lists apart), each of the 55 routes in `compat/hidden-routes-12.4.txt`, and two routes outside the document: `/socket`, the WebSocket Jellyfin serves and jellyfin-web opens, and `/System/Configuration/encoding`, the one key of `/System/Configuration/{key}` that clients read, which jellyscale serves by its own route. `compat/spec-operations-12.4.txt` lists its operations, one `METHOD path` per line, generated from the document; `compat/schema-12.4.json` keeps its types and response schemas (see Client coverage). Columns are method, path, class, slice, served_by, web_caller and evidence.

- Class is `done` (implemented or an alias in `compat/endpoints.yaml`), `stub-ok` (an empty answer is the final behaviour), `needed` (a client or feature above needs it) or `out-of-scope` (a non-goal above, or nothing calls it).
- Slice is the slice that delivers a `needed` route, or a `stub-ok` route that is not built yet; otherwise `-`.
- web_caller is `-` (no call found), `sdk` (a name match, see the search rule) or `ApiClient.<name>`, the legacy call a page makes.
- served_by is `-`, or for a `done` row that a different pattern answers, that route as `METHOD path`. The method must be the row's, except that a HEAD row may be served by the GET pattern.

`go test ./compat` fails when: a spec operation or hidden route has no row, or a row is in neither list (so a dropped or mistyped row fails); the file and the API's route list disagree about what is built; a `served_by` names the wrong method or a path that cannot serve the row; a `web_caller` is malformed; an obsolete route is `needed` without an `ApiClient.<name>` web_caller; the parental-ratings route leaves `phase5-library`; a slice has work and no group in Features; or the counts below are wrong.

Routes still to build, by slice (checked against the rows):

- `phase3`: 0 routes to build
- `phase4-ha`: 10 routes to build
- `identity`: 31 routes to build
- `phase5-library`: 45 routes to build
- `phase6-k8s`: 0 routes to build
- `phase7-syncplay`: 23 routes to build

A slice is complete when its count is 0 and its Features group has no **not built** line. Features without a route (HEVC, AV1, tone mapping, image and external subtitles, chapters, hardware encoding, trickplay generation) are not measured by the rows; their status is a claim by hand, to be proved by the integration tests of the slice. `phase6-k8s` has no routes (it is a chart, metrics and migration), so only its Features group counts.

How the evidence was gathered, and its limits:

- Search rule, stated once. A route counts as called by jellyfin-web 12.4.0 (`web-dist`) when its SDK method name appears there as `.name(` (`~N SDK call sites`), or when a page calls the legacy `ApiClient` (`ApiClient.name(`, or `getUrl("Path/...")` followed by `ajax` or `getJSON`), which the SDK count does not see; the row's web_caller names it. A definition inside `node_modules.@jellyfin.sdk` or `node_modules.jellyfin-apiclient` is not a call: only a page chunk calling it counts, so a method nothing calls (`getSearchHints`, `getPerson`, `getStudio`) leaves its route out of scope unless a client in scope matches its name. The SDK count is by method name, so a short or common name (`play`, `getYear`) over-counts; a name that something else in the bundle also uses is a false positive, and the row says so. Either way it says a call exists somewhere in the bundle, often on a dashboard page, not that a viewer reaches it.
- `compat/captures/jellyfin-web-12.4.json` says whether a real session exercised the route.
- The `clients` field is a match of the operation's name in the source of jellyfin-androidtv, Swiftfin and Streamyfin (default branches cloned on 2026-11-02). It is a name match, not a trace of a call, and acceptable as evidence when labelled so; a route no one matches is "none", not proof it is unused.
- A route nothing matched is `out-of-scope` only when its row says what was checked; a reason that is not a Non-goal bullet above is a bug in the row. A route that jellyfin-web calls on a viewer page is `needed`, never out of scope.
- Rows that say `observed on jellyfin/jellyfin:12.4 (empty server)` were requested from a disposable `jellyfin/jellyfin:12.4` container on a Docker-assigned port (2026-11-02). It had no library, so it shows existence and shape, not behaviour with data. Rows that do not say it were not observed.
- Rows in `needed` found only by a client name match carry `phase5-library` as a default slice, and the row says so; a maintainer should confirm those.

- Access to every route (`docs/access.md`): **done**. Every route declares who may call it and one test asks each. A client must send its token on every media request (film bytes, playlists, segments, subtitles, fonts, download; pictures by id excepted); this is stricter than Jellyfin on purpose, which opens some of these to a caller with no token.
- Failed sign-ins (`docs/access.md`, JS-145, D-020): **done**. Wrong passwords are counted per name (20), per known device (10) and per address (10, when `trustedProxies` is declared), each over 15 minutes, and a refused sign-in is 403 with `Retry-After`; no account is disabled and a token already issued is never touched.
