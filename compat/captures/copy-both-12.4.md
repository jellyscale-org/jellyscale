# What Jellyfin 12.4 does when a client plays both codecs of a film but not its container

Observed, not read from source. Server: `jellyfin/jellyfin:12.4` (jellyfin-ffmpeg), one library of 20 synthetic films in `copy-both-12.4.json` (same directory), names as there. All are progressive, 640x360, 24 fps, 20 s, keyframe every 72 frames: H.264 in Matroska with AAC stereo, AAC 5.1, AC3 stereo, AC3 5.1, E-AC3 5.1, Opus, FLAC, MP3 and TrueHD 5.1; H.264 in MP4 with AC3; HEVC in Matroska with AC3 stereo, AC3 5.1, AAC and Opus; and five films with two audio tracks (AC3 then AAC; AAC then AAC in MKV and in MP4; AAC then Opus; HEVC with AC3 then AAC). A sixth, H.264 and AAC in MPEG-TS, was also asked; jellyscale makes no index for it and it is not in the JSON. The PlaybackInfo body is the one jellyfin-web sends (`IsPlayback`, `AutoOpenLiveStream`, `AudioStreamIndex`, `SubtitleStreamIndex "-1"`, `MaxStreamingBitrate 1440987881` unless noted, `MediaSourceId`). The master and main playlists and segment 0 were fetched from the `TranscodingUrl`, and the ffmpeg command each run started is from Jellyfin's `FFmpeg.*.log` (`ffmpegVideo`, `ffmpegAudio`, `ffmpegLog` in the JSON).

Profiles (in the JSON as `deviceProfiles`), all constructed here from the captured ones, because jellyfin-web in Chrome plays Matroska and MP4 and so never meets a container it cannot play:

- `chrome`: jellyfin-web 12.4 in Chrome 154 (`transcode-12.4.md`). fMP4 HLS audio list `aac,mp2,opus,flac`, two channels.
- `chrome-nomkv`: `chrome` with the Matroska direct-play profile removed, so the container is what forces the stream.
- `surround`, `surround-nomkv`: the same with the fMP4 HLS list `aac,mp3,ac3,eac3,flac,opus` and six channels (`audio-copy-12.4.md`).
- `hevc-nomkv`, `hevc-surround-nomkv`, `hevc-surround`: `hevc-client` (`hevc-av1-12.4.md`) without Matroska direct play, the last with the surround list and with Matroska.

## What Jellyfin does

- **Both streams are copied when the client's fMP4 HLS profile lists the audio codec, its channels fit, and the video is copied.** The log is `FFmpeg.Remux-*` and the command has `-codec:v:0 copy -codec:a:0 copy` (`-bsf:v h264_mp4toannexb` or `hevc_mp4toannexb -tag:v:0 hvc1`), `-f hls -hls_segment_type fmp4`. H.264 with AAC stereo, Opus or FLAC under `chrome-nomkv`; the same with AC3, E-AC3 and 5.1 AAC under `surround-nomkv`; HEVC under the `hevc-` profiles.
- **When the video copies and the audio does not fit the profile, it is `FFmpeg.DirectStream-*`**: video copied, audio `libfdk_aac -ac 2 -ab 256000` (AC3 and 5.1 under `chrome-nomkv`; 5.1 AAC under `chrome-nomkv` adds `AudioChannelsNotSupported`). This is what jellyscale does today.
- **The video is re-encoded** (`FFmpeg.Transcode-*`, `libx264`) when the profile does not list the video codec (HEVC under `chrome-nomkv`), or when the cap is below the file's bitrate (`ContainerBitrateExceedsLimit`; 1.5 Mbps here); the audio is still copied if it fits. It also re-encoded the video of the TrueHD film with the audio, which jellyscale does not (JS-025 copies the video whatever the audio).
- **`TranscodeReasons`** are the direct-play check's: `ContainerNotSupported` for Matroska under `*-nomkv`, plus `AudioCodecNotSupported` when the direct-play profile lacks the codec even though the HLS profile takes it (AC3 under `surround-nomkv`: `ContainerNotSupported,AudioCodecNotSupported`, and a Remux). For an MP4 with AC3 under `surround-nomkv`: `AudioCodecNotSupported` alone, a Remux.
- **The master playlist** names the copied audio: `CODECS="avc1.42401E,ac-3"` (`mp4a.40.2`, `ac-3`, `ec-3`, `Opus`, `fLaC`, `mp4a.40.34` for MP3); `hvc1.1.4.L63.B0` for HEVC. `AudioBitrate` is the track's own bitrate (192000 for AC3 stereo, 448000 for 5.1, 960000 FLAC, the Matroska ones Jellyfin's guesses) and `VideoBitrate` is the cap minus it (`1440987881 - 192000 = 1440795881`). In `main.m3u8` and the segments `AudioCodec=copy`.
- **A track other than the default** on a file the client plays as it is (Chrome, Matroska or MP4) is a transcode with the reason **`SecondaryAudioNotSupported`**, and Jellyfin copies both streams when the track is AAC or Opus (`Two Ac3Aac Mkv`, `Two AacAac Mp4`, `Two AacOpus Mkv` track 2): `FFmpeg.Remux`, `-map 0:0 -map 0:2 -codec:v:0 copy -codec:a:0 copy`. The default track plays as it is where Chrome takes it. This is the case jellyfin-web in CI's Chrome meets: a viewer who picks the AAC track of an H.264 film whose default track is AC3. With HEVC under `chrome` (which lists no HEVC) the video is re-encoded and the AAC copied.

## Where jellyscale differs, and what this task changes

- **Today** jellyscale copies the video and re-encodes the audio whatever the audio is (`Source.copyAudio` returns false for a copied video; `TestVideoCopyNeverCopiesAudio`). This task copies both where Jellyfin does and as `copyAudio` already decides it for a re-encode (codec in the URL's list, channels within the limit, at most a quarter of the cap).
- **MP3 in Chrome**: Jellyfin delivers MPEG-TS because only the TS profile lists MP3; jellyscale makes fMP4 only (DOMAINS 21), so under `chrome-nomkv` the MP3 is re-encoded and the video copied. Under `surround` MP3 is copied with the video, `CODECS` naming it `mp4a.6B` (`TestMasterNamesTheCopiedAudio`), not `mp4a.40.34`.
- **TrueHD**: the video is copied, as since JS-025.
- **`SecondaryAudioNotSupported`**: jellyscale names no reason for a transcode forced only by the track choice (`TestDecideCopiesVideoTheClientPlays`, "another audio track"); this task leaves that as it is. The checks drop the reason from Jellyfin's list.
- **The URL's `AudioCodec`** stays the profile's list; Jellyfin writes the source's codec (`audio-copy-12.4.md`, difference 1).
- **Reasons**: where the container is the reason, Jellyfin also names `AudioCodecNotSupported` when the direct-play profile does not list the audio codec. jellyscale names the container alone today (Matroska with AC3 under `surround-nomkv`); `TestCopyReasonsAreJellyfinsAtAuto` asks for Jellyfin's list at Auto.
